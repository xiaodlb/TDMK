### Recurrence

| Topic | Delegated rule | Before/after example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anchors | A repeating task requires a start date. Daily means each local calendar day from that date; weekly means the start date's weekday every week; monthly means the start date's day number every month. Completion never moves the anchor. Generate only dates due through today, not future task records; future reminders may be prepared from the schedule. | Before: a weekly task starts Monday October 12, 2026 and is completed Wednesday October 14. After: the next occurrence is due Monday October 19, not October 21. A daily start October 12 yields October 12, 13, 14; a future start October 20 creates no active occurrence on October 12. |
| Missing monthly day | Use the last day of a shorter month while retaining the original day-number anchor. | Before: monthly start January 31, 2026. After: occurrences fall February 28, March 31, April 30. In leap year 2028 February uses February 29; February's clamp never changes March to the 28th/29th. |
| Catch-up and identity | Reconcile every missed scheduled date from the start (or last reconciled date) through today. Each slot has a stable identity independent of edited dates, completion, and timezone. Preserve completed occurrences and deletion/edit exclusions; never duplicate them. Catch-up can be processed in batches without discarding intervals. Each new occurrence copies the applicable template with all subtasks initially unchecked. Older unfinished occurrences remain overdue. | Before: daily October 12 occurrence exists unfinished; app is unused October 13–15. After opening October 15: exactly one each for October 13, 14, 15, with fresh unchecked checklists; October 12 stays overdue. Reopening again creates none. A deleted October 14 slot stays absent. |
| Edit one occurrence | Editing one changes only that occurrence, including its date, reminder, checklist, title, priority, note, or list. Preserve its original slot identity so reconciliation does not replace it. Removing its date makes it undated and clears its reminder, without stopping the series. Completed occurrences can be edited without automatic reopening except that unchecking or adding an unchecked subtask reopens the parent under the completion gate. Other edits preserve completion timestamp/day. Explicit reopening preserves date/checklist and changes only this occurrence. | Before: daily October 12 occurrence completed with A checked, October 13 unfinished. After moving the completed October 12 occurrence to October 16: it remains completed under its original completion day; October 13 and the schedule stay unchanged. Reopening it then makes it unfinished with October 16 retained and A checked. Unchecking A reopens only that occurrence. No second October 12 slot is generated. |
| Edit series | Series changes apply prospectively from today's local date, not from the selected occurrence's historical date. Preserve all completed occurrences, past unfinished occurrences, and single-occurrence overrides/exclusions. Update generated uncompleted, unmodified slots dated today to template field edits. Schedule/start-date changes replace unmodified unfinished slots from today onward with the new schedule; its new start date must be today or later. Preserve overrides as independent exceptions and retain old slot exclusions. Disabling repetition stops future generation, retaining existing occurrences. No series edit rewrites completed history or completion timestamps. | Before: on October 14 a daily series has October 11 completed, October 13 overdue, October 14 unmodified unfinished, and an October 12 single-occurrence override moved to October 20. After selecting the completed October 11 record and changing the series to weekly starting October 16: October 11 and 13 stay unchanged, unmodified October 14 is removed from the superseded schedule, October 20 override remains, and October 16/23 become the new weekly slots when due. A title-only series edit updates the unmodified October 14 record but preserves historical and overridden records. |
| Delete one | After confirmation, delete only the chosen occurrence, whether unfinished or completed, and retain an exclusion for its original slot to prevent regeneration. Cancel its reminder. Other records and the template remain. | Before: completed October 12 and unfinished October 13 belong to a daily series. After confirmed deletion of October 12: it disappears from Log and active views, stays absent after reconciliation, and October 13 and later slots remain. Canceling confirmation changes nothing. |
| Delete series | Confirmation explicitly says all occurrences, including completed history and overrides, will be deleted. Delete the template, all its occurrences, reminders, and associated exclusions atomically. No generation resumes. Cancel retains everything. | Before: a daily series contains completed October 12, overdue October 13, and an override moved to October 20. After confirmed series deletion: all three and the series disappear, including October 12 from Log. Reconciliation cannot recreate them. |

Deleting a custom list must also stop recurrence templates owned by that list and remove their occurrences, including moved overrides, with those consequences stated in the confirmation; otherwise list deletion could silently recreate tasks. Before: a Work series has an override moved to Personal. After confirmed Work deletion: that series and its override are gone; unrelated Personal tasks remain. Series field edits that move the series to another list preserve existing past/completed/overridden records in their own lists: moving its template Work to Personal today leaves yesterday's completed Work occurrence in Work and puts future slots in Personal.

Equal creation timestamps use stable task identity ascending as a final deterministic tie-breaker without changing the owner's oldest-created-first ordering. Before: two Medium tasks have identical creation timestamps with IDs A and B. After: A precedes B deterministically; any older Medium task still precedes both. A task without subtasks can be completed because no unchecked subtasks exist (logical consequence of the approved gate); adding an unchecked subtask to a completed parent reopens it just like unchecking one.

Schedule revisions remain part of the same series identity. A new schedule cannot regenerate a date already represented by a preserved completed record, override's original slot, or deletion exclusion in that series. Before: today's occurrence was completed, then the series is revised with today as its new start. After: today's completed record stays unique; generation begins at the next eligible slot. A moved override reserves its original slot, not its displayed date; distinct slots can legitimately have the same displayed date. Reopening makes an occurrence unfinished and removes it from the completed Log until completed again; completing again uses the new completion day, without altering other occurrences.

A future-start series can be edited before it starts. Before: create on October 13 with start October 20, then change its title October 14. After: the title change is saved prospectively, no occurrence exists before October 20, and the future schedule uses the new title. Instead change its start on October 14 to October 14: that day is eligible under the new schedule, without waiting for October 20. The initial revision boundary is min(start date, creation local day), while the anchor still controls the first slot. This 2026-10-13 technical correction was made under the owner's delegation to preserve the already resolved prospective-edit policy, not individually selected by the owner; [conflict record](https://github.com/xiaodlb/TDMK/issues/12#issuecomment-6133534566).

### Reminders and local time

Task dates are calendar dates; reminder times are local wall-clock times on those dates, interpreted in the device's current timezone. Reminder delivery is a single notification attempt per configured occurrence/time, not a priority-based repeated alert. Newly generated recurring occurrences copy the template's optional reminder time. A prepared reminder for a future slot reconciles that due occurrence before checking completion/exclusion and delivery, so it does not require a future active task record. Single-occurrence overrides and completed records remain independent of series reminder edits.

| Topic | Delegated rule | Before/after example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Past time and missed reminders | Save a selected time even if already past, label it missed, and do not immediately alert merely because it was created, edited, reopened, or permission was restored. An already armed reminder delivered late may alert once only if its scheduled local date is still today, it is unfinished, notifications are available, and no attempt has been consumed. Reconciliation on resume/reboot does not replay past times. Dates before today are missed, not re-armed daily. | Before: at 15:00 create today's task with 14:00 reminder. After: saved as missed, no instant alert. An alarm armed earlier for 14:00 but delivered at 15:00 may alert once; delivered tomorrow it is suppressed. Opening after three missed daily intervals creates overdue tasks without three catch-up alerts. |
| Completion, deletion, reopening | Completion/deletion cancels pending alarms and dismisses that task's notification. Reopening restores only a still-future, unconsumed reminder; past or already attempted reminders do not replay. An explicitly changed future date/time is a new reminder configuration. | Before: today's 18:00 reminder is armed; task completed at 16:00. After: no 18:00 alert. Reopen at 17:00 before any attempt: restore 18:00. Reopen at 19:00: missed, no alert. Reopen after its 18:00 alert: no duplicate. |
| Date changes or removal | Changing a date cancels the previous alarm and retains the chosen time on the new date. Arm if the new time is future; otherwise show missed. Removing the date clears the reminder; adding a date later does not silently restore it. | Before: October 12 at 18:00 is armed. After moving to October 13: cancel October 12 and arm October 13 at 18:00. Move to October 11: missed, no immediate alert. Remove the date: reminder is removed; adding October 14 requires explicitly choosing a new reminder. |
| Clock/timezone change | Preserve task dates and reminder wall-clock times, recalculate future instants in the new device zone, and refresh date-sensitive views. Cancel obsolete alarms before replacement; future unconsumed reminders re-arm, past ones become missed without immediate replay. A consumed reminder never repeats when the clock moves backward. Reconcile recurrence by date/slot identity, including forward day jumps, without duplicating on backward jumps. | Before: October 13 at 09:00 is armed in UTC+5. After moving to UTC+3 while it is still future: it alerts at 09:00 in UTC+3, not at 07:00. If the new local time is already 10:00: missed. Move the clock back after delivery: no second alert. |
| DST boundaries | A nonexistent local time shifts forward by the gap; an ambiguous repeated time uses the earlier instant once. The saved requested time stays unchanged for later dates. | Before: reminder March 8, 2026 at 02:30 in America/New_York (spring gap). After: resolved to 03:30 that day. November 1, 2026 at 01:30 uses the first 01:30 only, not both; the next normal date uses its requested time. |
| Notification or channel denial | Keep task/reminder data usable offline. Show a clear blocked status and a user-initiated route to system settings/request permission; do not repeatedly prompt or bypass denial. At the due time, suppress delivery and consume that attempt. After grant/re-enabling, arm only future unconsumed reminders; no backlog alerts. Channel blocking and app-level denial have the same product outcome. | Before: October 13 at 09:00 reminder saved with notifications blocked. After: task remains, UI says notifications blocked; no notification at 09:00. Grant at 10:00: no replay. A future October 14 reminder becomes eligible. |
| Exact-alarm permission unavailable | Prefer an exact one-shot alarm for a user-selected reminder when supported/authorized. If access is unavailable or revoked, retain data and use a permitted inexact alarm with visible approximate-timing status, never promise punctual delivery. Notification denial still blocks delivery. Re-check on resume and permission changes; replace fallback with exact only for future unconsumed reminders. | Before: 18:00 reminder saved without exact-alarm access but notifications allowed. After: approximate alarm remains, possibly delayed by Android; it may notify once later that same day. If delayed to the next day it is suppressed. Grant access at 17:00: replace fallback with exact 18:00; grant at 19:00 after a missed time: do not replay. |

Android constraints checked against primary documentation on 2026-10-12: inexact alarms can be delayed by power restrictions; exact-alarm access must be checked and revocation cancels future exact alarms. These are OS limitations, not guarantees of device punctuality. Reconcile persisted future reminders after reboot/resume; alarm delivery and permission fallbacks require device validation in later implementation issues. See [Android alarm scheduling](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/alarms), [notification runtime permission](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/notifications/notification-permission), and [notification channels](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/notifications/channels).

### Search rules

Apply Unicode NFKC normalization, default full case folding (locale-independent, excluding Turkic-specific mappings), then NFKC again to query and each searchable field. Trim and collapse Unicode whitespace runs to one space for matching only. Match the normalized complete query as a literal contiguous substring within at least one field; do not concatenate fields, interpret regex, split into AND/OR tokens, strip accents, transliterate, or add fuzzy matches. Stored text/formatting is unchanged. These normalization choices are delegated product policy, consistent with [Unicode normalization](https://unicode.org/reports/tr15/) and [default case-fold data](https://www.unicode.org/Public/UCD/latest/ucd/CaseFolding.txt).

Examples: query PASSPORT matches Passport; Cyrillic ПЛАН matches план; composed café matches cafe followed by combining acute accent, but cafe does not match café. Full case folding makes STRASSE match Straße. Query buy milk matches body buy followed by a newline and milk. Query buy milk does not match title Buy plus body Milk in different fields; query milk.* is literal, not a regular expression. A whitespace-only query clears search and restores the ordinary tab content. No match shows No matches with a clear-query action, never creates content or changes saved data. Each tab owns its query; switching tabs does not apply one tab's query to another. Queries are session UI state and are not backed up.

### Individual deletion and recovery

All confirmed deletions below are permanent within the app: no trash, undo action, retention window, or automatic recovery (availability/expiry: none). State that plainly in confirmation. A manually exported earlier backup may recover its snapshot only through the replacement-restore policy below; it is not item-level undo. Canceling confirmation, Back, or dismissing a dialog keeps the data and alarms unchanged. Apply confirmed record/exclusion changes together, then cancel/dismiss the deleted records' reminders; stale callbacks must re-check that the current record exists before delivery. A storage transaction failure retains the old records/exclusions and schedules, reports failure, and does not claim deletion.

A failed provider write cannot be promised atomic or cleanly removable. Current-device reminder delivery after export is retained where known; a fresh offline device cannot know delivery after export on another device.
